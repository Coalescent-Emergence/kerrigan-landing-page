**ASAM levels of care are the numbers (1.0, 2.1, 2.5, 3.5) that programs, payers and regulators use to describe a treatment setting.** They tell you where treatment happens, how many hours a week, who is in the building, and what medical coverage stands behind it. Virginia Medicaid ARTS (Addiction and Recovery Treatment Services) is written to the 3rd edition (2013) of the ASAM Criteria today. A move to the 4th edition (2023) is announced and undated.

This article explains the levels from the rule text and from ASAM's own pages. It is not clinical or billing advice.

## In brief

1. The 3.x levels are residential, 4.0 is hospital inpatient, and 2.5 is explicitly nonresidential.
2. PHP (partial hospitalization program, Level 2.5) is a day program of 20 or more hours a week, not a hospital stay.
3. Level 2.5 sets a 20 hour weekly minimum. Level 3.1 sets five hours in addition to other services. In our reading, the residential levels add the 24-hour setting.
4. The 4th edition (2023) folds withdrawal management into the main levels, replaces the readiness to change dimension, and adds co-occurring enhanced levels.
5. Virginia Medicaid ARTS is on the 3rd edition (2013) today, with a 4th edition transition announced and undated. Licensing incorporates the 3rd edition by reference.

## What the levels are, and who owns them

The levels come from The ASAM Criteria, published by the American Society of Addiction Medicine. ASAM says the criteria are used "to determine the appropriate level of care across a continuum" ([ASAM, About The ASAM Criteria](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/about-the-asam-criteria)). The numbers group into four broad levels (1 through 4), and "decimal numbers express further gradations of intensity and types of care provided" ([ASAM, Third Edition](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/asam-criteria-3rd-edition)).

ASAM is not a regulator: "Public entities, including state and local regulatory bodies, and payers will determine if and when they want to implement" the criteria ([ASAM FAQ](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/criteria-faq)).

CMS (the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services) has pointed states the same direction. Its 2017 letter to state Medicaid directors asked states to show they use evidence-based guidelines "such as those published by the American Society of Addiction Medicine (ASAM)." The letter covers levels including outpatient, intensive outpatient, residential, inpatient and medically supervised withdrawal management ([CMS SMD 17-003](https://www.medicaid.gov/federal-policy-guidance/downloads/smd17003.pdf)).

So the level numbers are ASAM's, and the hours, staffing and licences attached to them are written by each state. Virginia's version is below.

## The levels, one at a time

The hours and staffing below are Virginia's regulatory definitions, in [12VAC35-105-20](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency35/chapter105/section20/) for licensing and 12VAC30-130 Part XX for Medicaid, because a Virginia program is held to those texts. Other states differ.

**Level 0.5, early intervention.** The ARTS manual lists "0.5 Early Intervention/Screening Brief Intervention and Referral to Treatment (SBIRT)" as the entry point ([ARTS Provider Manual, Chapter IV](https://vamedicaid.dmas.virginia.gov/sites/default/files/2025-07/ARTS%20Provider%20Manual%20Chapter%20IV%20%28updated%207.7.25%29_Final.pdf)). The manual covers it as early intervention and screening, ahead of the treatment levels.

**Level 1.0, outpatient.** Virginia defines it as "center-based substance abuse treatment delivered to individuals for fewer than nine hours of service per week for adults" (12VAC35-105-20). The licensing admission rule for 1.0 says "no more than nine hours" ([12VAC35-105-1790](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincodefull/title12/agency35/chapter105/partVII/)). The two texts overlap at nine, and the IOP definition below starts at nine.

**Level 2.1, intensive outpatient (IOP).** Structured treatment, mainly counseling and education, for "nine to 19 hours of services per week for adults" in Virginia's definition. Psychiatric and medical needs are generally handled by consultation and referral (12VAC35-105-20).

**Level 2.5, partial hospitalization (PHP).** The next section covers this level in full, because the name misleads people.

**Level 3.1, clinically managed low-intensity residential.** A residential therapeutic environment for people who need structured support. Medicaid's rule requires "at least five hours per week of professionally directed treatment" ([12VAC30-130-5110](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency30/chapter130/section5110/)). The licensing rule adds that the five hours are "in addition to other treatment services offered to individuals, such as partial hospitalization or intensive outpatient treatment" ([12VAC35-105-1640](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincodefull/title12/agency35/chapter105/partVII/)). The rules also require awake staff onsite around the clock, and physician consultation and emergency services available at all times.

**Level 3.3, clinically managed population-specific high-intensity residential.** Virginia's licensing definition describes "a structured recovery environment in combination with high-intensity clinical services" (12VAC35-105-20).

**Level 3.5, clinically managed high-intensity residential.** "24-hour supportive treatment" with "daily scheduled professional services," awake onsite supervision, and credentialed medical staff reachable "on site or by telephone 24 hours per day" (12VAC35-105-20; [12VAC30-130-5130](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency30/chapter130/section5130/)).

**Level 3.7, medically monitored intensive inpatient.** Defined as "24-hour care in a facility under the supervision of medical personnel" (12VAC35-105-20). Medicaid requires a physician to be able to assess the person "in person or via telemedicine within 24 hours of admission." It also requires a licensed nurse to complete an alcohol and drug focused assessment on admission ([12VAC30-130-5140](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency30/chapter130/section5140/)). ASAM adds that a hospital can deliver 3.7, though most 3.7 services are residential (ASAM FAQ).

**Level 4.0, medically managed intensive inpatient.** This is the hospital level. ASAM puts it plainly: "ASAM Level 4 is a hospital level of care" (ASAM FAQ). Medicaid names "acute care hospitals licensed by the Virginia Department of Health" as the setting, with physician management and primary nursing care "available 24 hours per day" ([12VAC30-130-5150](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency30/chapter130/section5150/)).

## What PHP is

**PHP (partial hospitalization program) is a day program of several hours a day. Because it is nonresidential, the person does not stay overnight in it.**

The name misleads people, and ASAM says so: "PHP is a misnomer as these services are not delivered in a hospital setting" (ASAM FAQ).

What Virginia requires:

- **Hours.** At least 20 a week. Virginia's licensing definition is "a short-term, nonresidential substance use treatment program provided for a minimum of 20 hours a week" (12VAC35-105-20). The Medicaid manual adds the daily shape: "with at least five service hours per service day of skilled treatment services" (ARTS Provider Manual, Chapter IV).
- **Staff.** An interdisciplinary team of credentialed addiction treatment professionals (CATPs) and certified substance abuse counselors (CSACs) (same manual).
- **Medical and psychiatric care.** By consult or referral, with formal agreements to provide a medical consult within 8 hours by telephone or within 48 hours in person or by telemedicine. Emergency services are available 24 hours a day ([12VAC30-130-5100](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency30/chapter130/section5100/)).

## How a 2.5 program differs from a 3.x program

Compare the hour minimums in the Virginia text. Level 2.5 requires 20 or more hours a week. Level 3.1 requires five hours a week of professionally directed treatment, in addition to other services. The 3.5 and 3.7 text we read sets no weekly hour minimum. Level 4.0 requires counseling "available 16 hours per day" (12VAC30-130-5150). Our reading: what the residential levels add is the 24-hour setting, with staff and medical coverage around the clock.

| | Level 2.5 (PHP) | Levels 3.1 to 3.5 (residential) | Level 3.7 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where the person sleeps | Not at the program; it is "nonresidential" | At the program; these are residential levels | At the facility, under medical supervision |
| Hours | 20+ per week, 5+ per service day | 3.1: 5+ hours per week, in addition to other services; 3.5: daily scheduled services, no weekly minimum stated; staff present 24 hours | 24-hour care; no weekly minimum stated |
| Staffing | Interdisciplinary team; emergency services by phone when the program is not in session | Awake onsite supervision 24 hours a day | Physician-overseen team plus nursing |
| Medical coverage | Consult by phone within 8 hours or in person or by telemedicine within 48 hours; emergency services 24/7 | Physician consultation and emergency services 24/7; at 3.5, credentialed medical staff on site or by phone around the clock | Physician assessment within 24 hours of admission; nurse assessment on admission |

Sources: [12VAC35-105-20](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency35/chapter105/section20/), [12VAC30-130-5100](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency30/chapter130/section5100/), [5110](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency30/chapter130/section5110/), [5130](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency30/chapter130/section5130/) and [5140](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency30/chapter130/section5140/). The rule texts name residential and 24-hour settings. "Where the person sleeps" is our plain-English reading of "residential," not a phrase the rules use.

Housing is its own subject, because a recovery residence is not a treatment level. We cover it in [how recovery housing works](/articles/how-recovery-housing-works/).

## What the 4th edition changed

ASAM released the Third Edition in 2013 and the Fourth Edition in 2023 ([ASAM, About The ASAM Criteria](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/about-the-asam-criteria)). The adult volume came out digitally in October 2023 and in print in December 2023 ([ASAM FAQ](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/criteria-faq)). ASAM's own list of what changed ([ASAM, Fourth Edition](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/asam-criteria-4th-edition)):

- **Withdrawal management moved into the main continuum.** The Third Edition's separate withdrawal management levels map to Fourth Edition levels: 1-WM to 1.7, 2-WM to 2.7, 3.2-WM to 3.5, and 3.7-WM to 3.7 (ASAM FAQ).
- **Readiness to change stopped being its own dimension.** The Third Edition's Dimension 4 "does not contribute independently to the recommended level of care" and is replaced by "Dimension 6: Person-Centered Considerations."
- **New co-occurring enhanced levels:** 1.5 COE, 1.7 COE, 2.5 COE, 2.7 COE, 3.5 COE, 3.7 COE and 4 Psychiatric, plus 3.7 BIO for programs with enhanced biomedical capabilities.
- **A new Level 1.0 for long-term remission monitoring.** Watch the numbering: in Virginia's rules, "Level 1.0" is outpatient treatment (12VAC35-105-20).
- **Risk ratings are built into the Dimensional Admission Criteria, and PHP gets a new label.** "High Intensity Outpatient is used to refer to Level 2.5" (ASAM FAQ).

The ASAM pages we read do not say what became of Level 3.3 in the Fourth Edition, so we do not claim anything about it.

## Which edition Virginia uses today

**Medicaid ARTS is written to the Third Edition, as of today.** The regulation points to the ASAM treatment criteria "Third Edition" ([12VAC30-130-5010](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency30/chapter130/section5010/)), and another section cites "(Third Edition, 2013)" ([12VAC30-130-5040](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency30/chapter130/section5040/)). The ARTS manual, last revised 7/7/2025, says "DMAS is utilizing the treatment criteria for addictive, substance-related conditions as published by the ASAM (third edition 2013)" (ARTS Provider Manual, Chapter IV). DMAS is the Department of Medical Assistance Services.

**A move is announced and not dated.** The DMAS 2026 to 2028 Quality Strategy lists it among "three primary initiatives" it says are planned, and says: "Virginia will transition service descriptions and expectations to the Fourth Edition of the ASAM continuum of care" ([DMAS Quality Strategy](https://www.dmas.virginia.gov/media/njopayhu/2026-2028-quality-strategy.pdf)). We found no effective date.

**Licensing incorporates the Third Edition by reference.** The chapter's list of documents incorporated by reference includes "The ASAM: Treatment for Addictive, Substance-Related and Co-Occurring Conditions, Third Edition" ([12VAC35-105, full chapter](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincodefull/title12/agency35/chapter105/)). Part VII's own text uses level numbers without naming an edition, for example admission criteria of "Level 3.7 of ASAM" ([12VAC35-105 Part VII](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincodefull/title12/agency35/chapter105/partVII/)). The levels it names are 1.0, 2.1, 2.5, 3.1, 3.3, 3.5, 3.7 and 4.0.

We found no licence category for a Fourth Edition level such as 2.7 or 3.7 BIO, so do not assume one exists.
