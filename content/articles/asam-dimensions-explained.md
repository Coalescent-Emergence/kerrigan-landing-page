You are looking at an intake sheet, a treatment plan template or a payer form, and it says "Dimension 4." Which Dimension 4? The answer depends on which edition of the ASAM Criteria the document was written against.

This article lays out the six ASAM dimensions as the 3rd edition (2013) and the 4th edition (2023) each define them, says exactly where the two differ, and names the edition Virginia's Medicaid rules cite. Every clinical or regulatory statement links to its source. It is not clinical, billing or legal advice. Where a rule decides something for your program, verify with your compliance officer or the payer.

## What the dimensions are for

The ASAM Criteria are ASAM's standards for placing patients in addiction treatment. ASAM describes them as "the most widely used and comprehensive set of standards for placement, continued service, and transfer of patients" with addiction and co-occurring conditions. The standards were first published in 1991 as the ASAM patient placement criteria, the name changed with the Third Edition in 2013, and the Fourth Edition was released in 2023 ([ASAM, About The ASAM Criteria](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/about-the-asam-criteria)).

The dimensions are the assessment frame. ASAM says level of care recommendations and treatment plans "are developed based on multidimensional patient assessments," and that a patient entering treatment should get a standard assessment covering all six dimensions ([ASAM, About The ASAM Criteria](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/about-the-asam-criteria)). The 3rd edition page says the six dimensions "inform the level of care recommendation and the treatment plan" ([ASAM, 3rd edition](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/asam-criteria-3rd-edition)).

In short, the dimensions help decide where a person is placed, and the sources we cite below tie the treatment plan to the same assessment.

## The six dimensions in the 3rd edition (2013)

Virginia's Medicaid manual lists the six dimensions in its own wording, which follows the 3rd edition ([DMAS ARTS Provider Manual, Chapter IV, rev. 7/7/2025, p. 19](https://vamedicaid.dmas.virginia.gov/sites/default/files/2025-07/ARTS%20Provider%20Manual%20Chapter%20IV%20%28updated%207.7.25%29_Final.pdf)):

1. Dimension 1: Acute intoxication or withdrawal potential, or both
2. Dimension 2: Biomedical conditions and complications
3. Dimension 3: Emotional, behavioral, or cognitive conditions and complications
4. Dimension 4: Readiness to change
5. Dimension 5: Relapse, continued use, or continued problem potential
6. Dimension 6: Recovery/living environment

In plain terms, the first three ask what is happening in the body and mind right now. Dimension 4 asks where the person stands on change, and dimensions 5 and 6 ask what pulls them back toward use and what their surroundings support. That gloss is ours; the manual only lists the names.

The 3rd edition also includes "a framework for rating risks in each dimension," but its "dimensional admission criteria do not reference the risk ratings" ([ASAM, Criteria FAQ](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/criteria-faq)). That difference drives the next section.

## The six dimensions in the 4th edition (2023)

ASAM's 4th edition page says the six dimensions "were updated" and that the names were simplified "to reflect the field's evolving terminology" ([ASAM, 4th edition](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/asam-criteria-4th-edition)). The current names, from ASAM's main Criteria page ([ASAM, The ASAM Criteria](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria)):

1. Dimension 1: Intoxication, Withdrawal, and Addiction Medications
2. Dimension 2: Biomedical Conditions
3. Dimension 3: Psychiatric and Cognitive Conditions
4. Dimension 4: Substance Use-Related Risks
5. Dimension 5: Recovery Environment Interactions
6. Dimension 6: Person-Centered Considerations

Two things are different, and both matter for paperwork.

First, readiness to change left the list as its own dimension. ASAM says the 3rd edition's "Dimension 4: Readiness to Change does not contribute independently to the recommended level of care," and a new Dimension 6, Person-Centered Considerations, replaces it ([ASAM, 4th edition](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/asam-criteria-4th-edition)). The FAQ adds that readiness "is no longer included as a separate dimension" and should be considered "when assessing risks and needs within the other dimensions and subdimensions" ([ASAM, Criteria FAQ](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/criteria-faq)).

Second, the 4th edition describes subdimensions. ASAM says dimensions 1 through 5 are used to develop a level of care recommendation, while Dimension 6 is assessed with the patient "using a shared decision-making framework," covering barriers to care, including social determinants of health, and patient preferences ([ASAM, 4th edition](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/asam-criteria-4th-edition)).

We did not find a one-to-one map from the old dimensions 5 and 6 to the new 4 and 5 on ASAM's public pages. If a form uses the 4th edition names, read the edition itself before you treat "Dimension 5" in two documents as the same thing.

## Risk ratings: the difference that changes the decision rules

In the 3rd edition, risk ratings are a framework and the admission criteria do not reference them. In the 4th edition, ASAM says it "integrates risk ratings into the Dimensional Admission Criteria," and risk ratings within each subdimension "indicate the minimum level of care at which the patient should be treated" ([ASAM, Criteria FAQ](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/criteria-faq)).

After the assessor determines the subdimension ratings, the assessor applies the integrated Level of Care Determination Rules ([ASAM, Criteria FAQ](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/criteria-faq)). ASAM's Level of Care Assessment guide includes risk rating tables for dimensions 1 through 5 and a separate section for Dimension 6 ([ASAM, assessment guides](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/implementation-tools/Criteria-assessment-guides)).

We are describing structure here, not telling you how to rate anyone. The rating scales and rules live in the editions, and they are ASAM's to define.

## The continuum changed too

The dimensions feed a level of care, so the level numbers matter. ASAM's FAQ says 3rd edition Levels 1 WM, 2 WM, 3.2 WM and 3.7 WM map to 4th edition Levels 1.7, 2.7, 3.5 and 3.7, and that "High Intensity Outpatient is used to refer to Level 2.5" ([ASAM, Criteria FAQ](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/criteria-faq)). The 4th edition page describes "integration of medically managed levels of care" and a new Level 1.0 called "Long-Term Remission Monitoring" ([ASAM, 4th edition](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/asam-criteria-4th-edition)).

The new Level 1.0 creates a numbering collision. Virginia's licensing rules define "Substance abuse outpatient service" as "Level of care 1.0" ([12VAC35-105-20](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency35/chapter105/section20/)), and the ARTS rule lists "substance use outpatient services (ASAM Level 1.0)" ([12VAC30-130-5040 A.2](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency30/chapter130/section5040/)). Reading those two sources next to ASAM's description, our reading is that a Virginia document saying 1.0 and a 4th edition document saying 1.0 can point at different services, so check which edition a document means before you map one to the other.

## Which edition does Virginia use?

For Medicaid, Virginia's rule names the 3rd edition. The ARTS regulation says medical necessity is determined against "the most current version of the American Society of Addiction Medicine (ASAM) Treatment Criteria for Addictive, Substance-Related and Co-Occurring Conditions (Third Edition, 2013)" ([12VAC30-130-5040 B.2](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency30/chapter130/section5040/)); the purpose section also names the "Third Edition" ([12VAC30-130-5010](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency30/chapter130/section5010/)). The DMAS manual says the department "is utilizing the treatment criteria for addictive, substance-related conditions as published by the ASAM (third edition 2013)" ([ARTS Provider Manual, Chapter IV, rev. 7/7/2025, p. 15](https://vamedicaid.dmas.virginia.gov/sites/default/files/2025-07/ARTS%20Provider%20Manual%20Chapter%20IV%20%28updated%207.7.25%29_Final.pdf)).

The regulation also defines the assessment as including six dimensions, and its list includes "an individual's readiness to change" as item (iv), which fits the 3rd edition, although its wording ("home environment", "risks for relapse or continued use") differs from the manual's dimension names ([12VAC30-130-5020](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency30/chapter130/section5020/)). ARTS requires that the multidimensional assessment be completed "by a CATP or a CSAC under the supervision of a CATP" ([12VAC30-130-5040 A.1](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency30/chapter130/section5040/)). The manual says the level of care, the Individual Service Plan and recovery strategies "shall be based upon this multidimensional assessment" ([ARTS Provider Manual, p. 19](https://vamedicaid.dmas.virginia.gov/sites/default/files/2025-07/ARTS%20Provider%20Manual%20Chapter%20IV%20%28updated%207.7.25%29_Final.pdf)).

For licensing, Virginia's DBHDS rules use ASAM level numbers. Their admission criteria for each residential, partial hospitalization and outpatient level say a person must "Meet the admission criteria" of a given level "of ASAM," and Part VII itself names no edition ([12VAC35-105, Part VII](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincodefull/title12/agency35/chapter105/partVII/)). The chapter's list of documents incorporated by reference does name one: "The ASAM: Treatment for Addictive, Substance-Related and Co-Occurring Conditions, Third Edition" ([12VAC35-105, Documents Incorporated by Reference](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincodefull/title12/agency35/chapter105/)). So both the licensing chapter and the Medicaid rule point to the 3rd edition as written. If your program's licensing question turns on an edition, confirm with DBHDS.

A move is planned, with no date that we found. DMAS's 2026 to 2028 Quality Strategy says "Virginia will transition service descriptions and expectations to the Fourth Edition of the ASAM continuum of care" ([DMAS 2026-2028 Quality Strategy](https://www.dmas.virginia.gov/media/njopayhu/2026-2028-quality-strategy.pdf), SUD treatment gaps section, after p. 47). The ARTS manual library page lists Chapter IV as updated 7.7.25 and does not mention the move ([DMAS ARTS manual library](https://vamedicaid.dmas.virginia.gov/pdf_chapter/addiction-and-recovery-treatment-services)). As written, the regulation still names the 2013 edition. ASAM itself says it "is not a regulatory body" and that public entities and payers decide whether to implement the standards ([ASAM, Criteria FAQ](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/criteria-faq)). So the release of the 4th edition does not by itself change the edition the ARTS rule names, and the planned transition may change it later. Confirm the current edition with DMAS or the member's managed care plan before you rely on this page.

## Adolescents

ASAM publishes the 4th edition for adults and for adolescents in separate volumes ([ASAM, The ASAM Criteria](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria)). ASAM's FAQ says the digital and print versions of the Adolescents and Transition-Aged Youth volume "are now available" ([ASAM, Criteria FAQ](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/criteria-faq); [ASAM, adolescent volume](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/adolescent-volume)). The same FAQ still carries an older answer telling readers to continue using the Third Edition for adolescents while waiting for that volume, so check which edition a payer applies to adolescents.

## A short checklist for any form that says "ASAM"

1. Which edition does this form or template cite, by name?
2. Does it list readiness to change as a dimension? If yes, it follows the 3rd edition. If it lists Person-Centered Considerations as Dimension 6, it follows the 4th.
3. Which edition does the payer or regulator for this service name? For Virginia Medicaid ARTS, the rule text says the 3rd edition, 2013, and DMAS's Quality Strategy says Virginia will transition to the 4th, so ask for the current date.
4. Does a level number like 1.0 mean the same service in both documents?
5. Who is allowed to complete the assessment for this service, and who signs it?
