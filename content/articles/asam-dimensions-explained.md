**Virginia Medicaid and Virginia licensing both name the 3rd edition (2013) of the ASAM Criteria, the standards of the American Society of Addiction Medicine (ASAM). A move to the 4th edition (2023) is planned, with no date we found.** So when a form says "Dimension 4," the 3rd edition means readiness to change, and the 4th edition means substance use-related risks. Check which edition the form was written against.

This article lists the six ASAM dimensions in each edition, shows where the two differ, and gives the sources for Virginia's edition. It is not clinical, billing or legal advice.

## In brief

- Both editions have six dimensions, but the names and meanings of dimensions 4, 5 and 6 changed.
- Readiness to change was its own dimension in the 3rd edition. In the 4th it is folded into the others, and Dimension 6 is now Person-Centered Considerations.
- Only the 4th edition builds risk ratings into its admission criteria.
- A level number such as 1.0 can mean different services in Virginia's rules and in the 4th edition.
- For Virginia Medicaid ARTS (Addiction and Recovery Treatment Services), the rule text says the 3rd edition. The Department of Medical Assistance Services (DMAS) says Virginia will move to the 4th.

## What the dimensions are for

The dimensions are the assessment frame for placing a person in treatment. The treatment plan rests on the same assessment. ASAM says level of care recommendations and treatment plans "are developed based on multidimensional patient assessments," and that a patient entering treatment should get a standard assessment covering all six dimensions ([ASAM, About The ASAM Criteria](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/about-the-asam-criteria)). The 3rd edition page says the six dimensions "inform the level of care recommendation and the treatment plan" ([ASAM, 3rd edition](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/asam-criteria-3rd-edition)).

The ASAM Criteria are the American Society of Addiction Medicine's standards for placing patients in addiction treatment. ASAM describes them as "the most widely used and comprehensive set of standards for placement, continued service, and transfer of patients" with addiction and co-occurring conditions. They were first published in 1991 as the ASAM patient placement criteria. The name changed with the Third Edition in 2013, and the Fourth Edition was released in 2023 ([ASAM, About The ASAM Criteria](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/about-the-asam-criteria)).

## The six dimensions in the 3rd edition (2013)

Virginia's Medicaid manual lists the six dimensions in its own wording, which follows the 3rd edition ([DMAS ARTS Provider Manual, Chapter IV, rev. 7/7/2025, p. 19](https://vamedicaid.dmas.virginia.gov/sites/default/files/2025-07/ARTS%20Provider%20Manual%20Chapter%20IV%20%28updated%207.7.25%29_Final.pdf)):

1. Dimension 1: Acute intoxication or withdrawal potential, or both
2. Dimension 2: Biomedical conditions and complications
3. Dimension 3: Emotional, behavioral, or cognitive conditions and complications
4. Dimension 4: Readiness to change
5. Dimension 5: Relapse, continued use, or continued problem potential
6. Dimension 6: Recovery/living environment

In plain terms (this gloss is ours; the manual only lists the names), the first three ask what is happening in the body and mind right now. Dimension 4 asks where the person stands on change. Dimensions 5 and 6 ask what pulls them back toward use and what their surroundings support.

The 3rd edition also includes "a framework for rating risks in each dimension," but its "dimensional admission criteria do not reference the risk ratings" ([ASAM, Criteria frequently asked questions (FAQ)](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/criteria-faq)). That difference drives the section on risk ratings below.

## The six dimensions in the 4th edition (2023)

ASAM says the six dimensions "were updated" and that the names were simplified "to reflect the field's evolving terminology" ([ASAM, 4th edition](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/asam-criteria-4th-edition)). The current names are on ASAM's main Criteria page ([ASAM, The ASAM Criteria](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria)):

1. Dimension 1: Intoxication, Withdrawal, and Addiction Medications
2. Dimension 2: Biomedical Conditions
3. Dimension 3: Psychiatric and Cognitive Conditions
4. Dimension 4: Substance Use-Related Risks
5. Dimension 5: Recovery Environment Interactions
6. Dimension 6: Person-Centered Considerations

Two differences matter for paperwork.

**Readiness to change is no longer its own dimension.** ASAM says the 3rd edition's "Dimension 4: Readiness to Change does not contribute independently to the recommended level of care." A new Dimension 6, Person-Centered Considerations, replaces it ([ASAM, 4th edition](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/asam-criteria-4th-edition)). Readiness is now considered "when assessing risks and needs within the other dimensions and subdimensions" ([ASAM, Criteria FAQ](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/criteria-faq)).

**The 4th edition describes subdimensions.** Dimensions 1 through 5 are used to develop a level of care recommendation. Dimension 6 is assessed with the patient "using a shared decision-making framework," covering barriers to care, including social determinants of health, and patient preferences (ASAM, 4th edition).

We did not find a one-to-one map from the old dimensions 5 and 6 to the new 4 and 5 on ASAM's public pages. If a form uses the 4th edition names, read the edition itself before you treat "Dimension 5" in two documents as the same thing.

## Risk ratings: the difference that changes the decision rules

In the 3rd edition, risk ratings are a framework, and the admission criteria do not reference them. In the 4th edition, ASAM says it "integrates risk ratings into the Dimensional Admission Criteria." Risk ratings within each subdimension "indicate the minimum level of care at which the patient should be treated" ([ASAM, Criteria FAQ](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/criteria-faq)).

After the assessor determines the subdimension ratings, the assessor applies the integrated Level of Care Determination Rules (same FAQ). ASAM's Level of Care Assessment guide includes risk rating tables for dimensions 1 through 5 and a separate section for Dimension 6 ([ASAM, assessment guides](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/implementation-tools/Criteria-assessment-guides)).

This article describes structure, not how to rate anyone. The rating scales and rules live in the editions, and they are ASAM's to define.

## The continuum changed too

The dimensions feed a level of care, so the level numbers matter. ASAM's FAQ says 3rd edition Levels 1 WM (withdrawal management), 2 WM, 3.2 WM and 3.7 WM map to 4th edition Levels 1.7, 2.7, 3.5 and 3.7. It also says "High Intensity Outpatient is used to refer to Level 2.5" (ASAM, Criteria FAQ). The 4th edition page describes "integration of medically managed levels of care" and a new Level 1.0 called "Long-Term Remission Monitoring" (ASAM, 4th edition).

**The new Level 1.0 creates a numbering collision.** Virginia's licensing rules define "Substance abuse outpatient service" as "Level of care 1.0" ([12VAC35-105-20](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency35/chapter105/section20/)). The ARTS rule lists "substance use outpatient services (ASAM Level 1.0)" ([12VAC30-130-5040 A.2](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency30/chapter130/section5040/)). Our reading, from those two sources next to ASAM's description, is that a Virginia document saying 1.0 and a 4th edition document saying 1.0 can point at different services. Check which edition a document means before you map one to the other.

## Which edition does Virginia use?

**For Medicaid, Virginia's rule names the 3rd edition.** The ARTS regulation says medical necessity is determined against "the most current version of the American Society of Addiction Medicine (ASAM) Treatment Criteria for Addictive, Substance-Related and Co-Occurring Conditions (Third Edition, 2013)" ([12VAC30-130-5040 B.2](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency30/chapter130/section5040/)). The purpose section also names the "Third Edition" ([12VAC30-130-5010](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency30/chapter130/section5010/)).

The DMAS manual says the department "is utilizing the treatment criteria for addictive, substance-related conditions as published by the ASAM (third edition 2013)" ([ARTS Provider Manual, Chapter IV, rev. 7/7/2025, p. 15](https://vamedicaid.dmas.virginia.gov/sites/default/files/2025-07/ARTS%20Provider%20Manual%20Chapter%20IV%20%28updated%207.7.25%29_Final.pdf)).

The regulation also defines the assessment as including six dimensions. Its list includes "an individual's readiness to change" as item (iv), which fits the 3rd edition. Its wording ("home environment", "risks for relapse or continued use") differs from the manual's dimension names ([12VAC30-130-5020](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency30/chapter130/section5020/)).

ARTS requires that the multidimensional assessment be completed "by a CATP or a CSAC under the supervision of a CATP" ([12VAC30-130-5040 A.1](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency30/chapter130/section5040/)). A CATP is a credentialed addiction treatment professional, and a CSAC is a certified substance abuse counselor. The manual says the level of care, the Individual Service Plan and recovery strategies "shall be based upon this multidimensional assessment" ([ARTS Provider Manual, p. 19](https://vamedicaid.dmas.virginia.gov/sites/default/files/2025-07/ARTS%20Provider%20Manual%20Chapter%20IV%20%28updated%207.7.25%29_Final.pdf)).

**For licensing, the rules also point to the 3rd edition.** The Department of Behavioral Health and Developmental Services (DBHDS) licensing rules use ASAM level numbers. Their admission criteria for each residential, partial hospitalization and outpatient level say a person must "Meet the admission criteria" of a given level "of ASAM." Part VII itself names no edition ([12VAC35-105, Part VII](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincodefull/title12/agency35/chapter105/partVII/)).

The chapter's list of documents incorporated by reference does name one: "The ASAM: Treatment for Addictive, Substance-Related and Co-Occurring Conditions, Third Edition" ([12VAC35-105, Documents Incorporated by Reference](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincodefull/title12/agency35/chapter105/)). So the licensing chapter and the Medicaid rule both point to the 3rd edition as written. If your program's licensing question turns on an edition, confirm with DBHDS.

**A move to the 4th edition is planned. We found no date.** DMAS's 2026 to 2028 Quality Strategy says "Virginia will transition service descriptions and expectations to the Fourth Edition of the ASAM continuum of care" ([DMAS 2026-2028 Quality Strategy](https://www.dmas.virginia.gov/media/njopayhu/2026-2028-quality-strategy.pdf), SUD treatment gaps section, after p. 47).

Until that happens, the regulation as written names the 3rd edition, and the planned transition may change that:

- The ARTS regulation, as written, still names the 2013 edition.
- The ARTS manual library lists Chapter IV as updated 7.7.25 and does not mention the move ([DMAS ARTS manual library](https://vamedicaid.dmas.virginia.gov/pdf_chapter/addiction-and-recovery-treatment-services)).
- The 4th edition's release does not change this by itself. ASAM says it "is not a regulatory body." Public entities and payers decide whether to implement its standards (ASAM, Criteria FAQ).

Before you rely on this page, confirm the current edition with DMAS or the member's managed care plan.

## Adolescents

ASAM publishes the 4th edition for adults and for adolescents in separate volumes ([ASAM, The ASAM Criteria](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria)). ASAM's FAQ says the digital and print versions of the Adolescents and Transition-Aged Youth volume "are now available" ([ASAM, Criteria FAQ](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/criteria-faq); [ASAM, adolescent volume](https://www.asam.org/asam-criteria/adolescent-volume)).

The same FAQ still carries an older answer telling readers to continue using the Third Edition for adolescents while waiting for that volume. Check which edition a payer applies to adolescents.

## A short checklist for any form that says "ASAM"

1. Which edition does this form or template cite, by name?
2. Does it list readiness to change as a dimension? If yes, it follows the 3rd edition. If it lists Person-Centered Considerations as Dimension 6, it follows the 4th.
3. Which edition does the payer or regulator for this service name? For Virginia Medicaid ARTS, the rule text says the 3rd edition, 2013, and DMAS's Quality Strategy says Virginia will transition to the 4th, so ask for the current date.
4. Does a level number like 1.0 mean the same service in both documents?
5. Who is allowed to complete the assessment for this service, and who signs it?

If any answer depends on a rule, take it to your compliance officer or the payer.

For the level numbers themselves, see [ASAM levels of care explained](/articles/levels-of-care-explained/).
