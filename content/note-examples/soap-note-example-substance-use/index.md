You need to write a SOAP note for a client in substance use treatment and want to see the finished thing. This page explains the four parts and gives one complete example for a made-up client.

The client below is fictional. The name, dates and details are invented for teaching, and no real person or record is behind them. It shows structure, not clinical, billing or legal advice. Your program, payer and state set the required fields.

## What a SOAP note is

SOAP stands for Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan. Lawrence Weed developed the structure almost 50 years ago; it is one of the most widely used documentation methods for clinicians ([Podder, Lew and Ghassemzadeh, "SOAP Notes," StatPearls, 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482263/), abstract read through Europe PMC).

- **Subjective**: what the client reports, in their words. Symptoms, cravings, use since the last session, mood, and concerns.
- **Objective**: what you observe or measure. Appearance, behavior, screening scores, drug screen results, and vital signs if your role takes them.
- **Assessment**: your clinical reading of S and O together. Diagnosis in play, progress, risk.
- **Plan**: what happens next. Interventions, referrals, coordination with other providers, and the next appointment.

## Header fields to include

Add the fields your payer wants in every note. Virginia's Medicaid progress note rule, 12VAC30-60-185, lists the service name, the date, the signature and credentials of the provider, the setting and the time or units delivered ([Virginia Administrative Code, 12VAC30-60-185](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency30/chapter60/section185/)). Check your own state. The example carries all of them.

## Worked example: SOAP note, fictional client

**Client:** "Priya S." (fictional), adult, opioid use disorder, in outpatient treatment; takes buprenorphine prescribed by an outside prescriber
**Service:** Individual counseling
**Date:** 2026-11-12
**Setting:** Outpatient clinic office
**Duration:** 45 minutes (10:00 to 10:45 AM)

**S (Subjective):** Client reports no opioid use since the last session 7 days ago. She says cravings come "mostly in the evening, when I'm alone," and rates them 4 out of 10 on a typical day. She reports taking her medication as prescribed and says she missed no doses. She says her mood has been "okay, a little flat" and that she has not been going to her weekly mutual-help meeting because of a change in her work schedule. She says she is worried about telling her employer about her treatment.

**O (Objective):** Client arrived 5 minutes early. Alert and oriented, groomed, calm, cooperative. Speech and thought process were normal. No signs of intoxication or withdrawal observed. PHQ-9 administered today: 11, which is up from 8 at intake. The clinic's point-of-care urine screen collected before the session: negative for opioids other than buprenorphine, as expected. Counselor reviewed the client's treatment plan, which lists medication adherence, evening coping, and meeting attendance as goals.

**A (Assessment):** Client continues to do well on the plan's central goal of opioid abstinence, supported by self-report and the screen. Evening craving and the slight rise in PHQ-9 suggest a mild increase in depressive symptoms and a change in routine that dropped a protective factor, meeting attendance. No suicidal ideation reported on the PHQ-9 item or on direct inquiry. Risk of return to use is low to moderate. Employer concern is an environmental stressor to address.

**P (Plan):** Client will identify two alternative meeting times and bring them next session. Counselor and client built an evening schedule with two scheduled activities, to be tried this week. Counselor will repeat the PHQ-9 in two weeks and discuss with the prescriber if the score rises again, with client consent on file. Counselor will go over what a release of information to the employer would and would not allow, so the client can decide. Next individual session: 2026-11-19.

*Signed: [Counselor name, credentials], 2026-11-12*

## What makes this note strong

- **Subjective and Objective are kept apart.** Her words sit in S and your observations and numbers sit in O, so the Assessment can be traced to each.
- **It uses a measure.** A repeated score such as the PHQ-9 gives you a number to compare across sessions.
- **The Assessment explains a change.** It says why the score moved and what it means for risk.
- **The Plan is concrete.** Each step has an owner and a date.
- **Privacy is respected.** The note states that consent is on file before coordination with another provider, and does not copy more than the record needs.
