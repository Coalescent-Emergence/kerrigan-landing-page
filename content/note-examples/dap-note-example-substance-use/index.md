You have a session in front of you and a blank note. This page shows what a DAP note holds and gives one complete example for a made-up outpatient client, so you can compare it with your own.

The client below is fictional. The name, dates and details are invented for teaching. No real person or record is behind them. It is an example of structure, not clinical, billing or legal advice. Your program, payer and state set the required fields.

## What a DAP note is

DAP stands for Data, Assessment, Plan.

- **Data**: what happened in the session. What the client said and did, what you observed, and what you did. Subjective and objective material both go here, in one section.
- **Assessment**: what you make of the data. Progress or lack of it against the treatment plan goals, risk, and how the client is responding.
- **Plan**: what happens next. Homework, the next session, referrals, and any change to the plan.

DAP is the SOAP note with the subjective and objective parts merged into one section. That is the usual description of the format; we did not find a primary source that defines it, so treat this as common convention.

## Header fields to include

Many payers require a few fields in every note whatever the format. Virginia's Medicaid rule for progress notes, 12VAC30-60-185, lists the name of the service, the date, the signature and credentials of the person who rendered it, the setting, and the time or units delivered ([Virginia Administrative Code, 12VAC30-60-185](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency30/chapter60/section185/)). Other states and payers differ, so check yours. The example below carries all of these.

## Worked example: DAP note, fictional client

**Client:** "Jordan M." (fictional), adult, alcohol use disorder, moderate, in intensive outpatient treatment
**Service:** Individual counseling
**Date:** 2026-11-10
**Setting:** Outpatient clinic office
**Duration:** 50 minutes (2:00 to 2:50 PM)
**Treatment plan goal addressed:** Goal 2, "Identify triggers for drinking and use two coping skills when they occur."

**D (Data):** Client arrived on time. He reported one drinking episode since the last session, on Saturday, after an argument with his brother. He said he "had three beers before I realized what I was doing" and called his sponsor the next morning. He reported poor sleep (about four hours a night for three nights) and cravings that he rated 7 out of 10 on Saturday and 3 out of 10 today. Client was alert, oriented, and made good eye contact. Affect was tired and mildly anxious; speech was normal. He did not appear intoxicated. Session used a chain analysis of Saturday's episode. Client identified family conflict as the trigger, and named the point where he could have left the house. Counselor reviewed the urge-surfing skill taught last week; client said he had not tried it on Saturday. They rehearsed it in session for about five minutes, and he rated his craving 2 out of 10 afterward.

**A (Assessment):** Client had a brief return to use that he reported himself and responded to by calling his sponsor, which is a use of a coping skill from his plan. Family conflict is a clear trigger and sleep loss is a likely contributing factor. Insight is good and engagement is strong. Risk of continued use in the coming week is moderate because of the unresolved conflict with his brother. No suicidal or homicidal ideation reported. Progress on Goal 2 is partial: he identified triggers, and skills are not yet used at the moment of need.

**P (Plan):** Client will practice urge surfing once daily and log any cravings on the card provided. He will call his sponsor before, not after, entering a conflict he expects to be hard. Counselor will raise sleep with the client's prescriber at the next scheduled contact, with his written consent already on file. Next individual session is 2026-11-17; the group session on Thursday stays on the schedule. Counselor will revisit Goal 2 and ask about the log at the next session.

*Signed: \[Counselor name, credentials\], 2026-11-10*

## What makes this note strong

- **It is specific.** Quotes, ratings and times separate this client from every other client on the caseload. Virginia's Medicaid manual asks that each note "demonstrate unique differences particular to individuals' circumstances, treatment and progress" ([DMAS ARTS Provider Manual, Chapter IV](https://vamedicaid.dmas.virginia.gov/sites/default/files/2025-07/ARTS%20Provider%20Manual%20Chapter%20IV%20%28updated%207.7.25%29_Final.pdf)).
- **It ties to the treatment plan.** The note names the goal and says whether progress is partial or complete.
- **Data and Assessment stay apart.** Facts sit in D and the clinical judgment sits in A, so a reader can see what each rests on.
- **The Plan is something you can check next week.** It names who does what and when.
- **It records risk.** The note says what was asked and what the client answered.
