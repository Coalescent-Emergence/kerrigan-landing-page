Progress notes are the notes you write most often, and the ones a payer or auditor reads first. This page lists what they need and shows one complete example for a made-up client.

The client below is fictional. The name, dates and details are invented for teaching, and no real person or record is behind them. It shows structure, not clinical, billing or legal advice. Rules differ by state and payer, so check yours.

## What a progress note is

A progress note records one service and how the client responded to it. A progress note is often defined by what it must show rather than by fixed headings. Virginia's Medicaid addiction treatment manual defines progress notes as "individual-specific documentation that contains the unique differences particular to the individual's circumstances, treatment and progress" that is "signed and dated," and says they "shall disclose the extent of services provided and corroborate the units billed" ([DMAS ARTS Provider Manual, Chapter IV, rev. 7/7/2025](https://vamedicaid.dmas.virginia.gov/sites/default/files/2025-07/ARTS%20Provider%20Manual%20Chapter%20IV%20%28updated%207.7.25%29_Final.pdf)). The same manual says progress notes "should document the member's response to the therapeutic services provided and how the member's treatment plan has been modified over time."

The minimum content in the Virginia rule is the name of the service, the date, the signature and credentials of the person who rendered it, the setting, and the time or units delivered ([Virginia Administrative Code, 12VAC30-60-185](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title12/agency30/chapter60/section185/)).

One fact to keep in mind: HIPAA's definition of psychotherapy notes, which can be kept apart from the record, excludes session start and stop times, modalities and frequencies, results of clinical tests, and summaries of diagnosis, functional status, treatment plan, symptoms, prognosis and progress to date ([45 CFR 164.501](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-45?part=164&section=164.501)). Those items belong in your progress note.

## Fields to include

1. Client, date, service, setting, start and stop time or duration
2. The treatment plan goal the session addressed
3. What you did (intervention)
4. How the client responded
5. Progress toward the goal, and any risk
6. Plan: next steps and next appointment
7. Signature, credentials, date

## Worked example: progress note, fictional client

**Client:** "Tomás V." (fictional), adult, stimulant use disorder, in outpatient treatment
**Service:** Individual counseling
**Date:** 2026-11-11
**Setting:** Outpatient clinic office
**Duration:** 50 minutes (4:00 to 4:50 PM)
**Treatment plan goal addressed:** Goal 1, "Reduce use to zero days per week as measured by self-report and weekly screens."

**Intervention:** Counselor used motivational interviewing to review the client's week and explore his mixed feelings about stopping. Counselor and client completed a relapse prevention worksheet that lists his three highest-risk situations (payday, time alone at home, contact with a former using friend).

**Client response:** Client was engaged and open. He reported three days of use in the past week, down from five the week before, and said he used on payday "because I had money in my pocket and nothing planned." He stated, "I want to stop, I just don't know how to get through Friday." He identified one safe activity for payday, a standing basketball game, and agreed to ask a family member to hold part of his paycheck.

**Progress toward goal:** Partial. Days of use have decreased for two weeks in a row, but the goal of zero days is not yet met. Client's readiness appears to be growing. No safety concerns were identified. Risk of use on the next payday is high without a plan.

**Plan:** Client will go to the basketball game on Friday and will ask his sister about holding part of his paycheck. Counselor will check on both at the next session and review the screen result. Next individual session: 2026-11-18. Group continues Thursdays.

*Signed: [Counselor name, credentials], 2026-11-11*

## What makes this note strong

- **It links the session to a plan goal.** The first line names Goal 1 and reads against it.
- **It shows the service.** Date, setting, duration and intervention support the units billed.
- **It shows the client's response.** The note has his words and what he agreed to do.
- **It says where progress stands.** "Partial" with the evidence for it is more useful than "doing well."
- **It looks ahead.** The plan names the next appointment and what you will ask about.
